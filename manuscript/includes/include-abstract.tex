Atom mapping is often treated as if every reaction had a single correspondence between reactant and product atoms. In practice, the same endpoints can support multiple plausible mappings, while molecular symmetry can generate many equivalent relabelings that add complexity without adding new chemistry. We introduce MAPA, a graph-based atom-mapping method designed to preserve meaningful alternatives without explicitly enumerating symmetry-equivalent permutations. MAPA grows matched fragments between reactants and products, branches when distinct fragment placements remain possible, and represents symmetry-related atom assignments as compact, correlated families. A cut-sweep strategy perturbs fragment growth to recover alternatives that greedy search would otherwise discard, while a symbolic decoder translates the resulting mapping families into distinct bond-event patterns. Across the Golden benchmark, MAPA recovers annotated correspondences more consistently than the comparison methods evaluated here while retaining alternative mappings in an inspectable representation. On reactions with coordinates and Wiberg bond orders, it also reveals bond-event alternatives that can be missed when a single mapping is selected or when mappings are chosen only by minimizing the number of bond changes. Rather than forcing a reaction into one correspondence too early, MAPA preserves the meaningful possibilities so they can be examined and interpreted in subsequent chemical analysis.
